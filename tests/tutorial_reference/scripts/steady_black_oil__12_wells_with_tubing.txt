  • Well 3's static oil-and-water column (25 °API, WOR 0.8, 2 000 m) weighs
    more than its reservoir pressure: it cannot flow without artificial lift.
  • Larger tubing reduces friction in the high-rate wells; the Well model
    solves each well's tubing on its own, so the network converges in a
    few outer iterations even with 2 km two-phase risers.
  • The reservoir fluid warms slightly at the sandface (liquid expanding at
    constant enthalpy) and then cools along the tubing; the slower well
    arrives colder.  Wellhead temperature matters downstream: viscosity,
    wax and hydrate margins all start from it.
  • Hagedorn-Brown suits oil wells, Gray gas and gas-condensate wells; the
    spread between correlations is part of the uncertainty of any well model.
  • Raising the separator pressure lifts the manifold above Well 2's maximum
    wellhead pressure: Well 2 dies against the manifold while Well 1, with
    more reservoir pressure and gas, keeps producing.
  • A well whose curve peaks at a finite rate (a gassy well like Well 1) cannot
    flow below that rate.  Between its static wellhead pressure and that peak
    it is bistable: dead or producing.  If producing would lift the manifold
    above the peak, the well stays dead — shut in, it cannot start against
    the manifold (it would need unloading or gas lift).  result.well_states
    gives each well's state and the reason.
  • The nodal analysis cuts the network at Well 1's wellhead: the well's own
    curve (inflow) against the pressure the rest of the network needs to take
    each rate (outflow).  They cross where the network solve puts the well.
